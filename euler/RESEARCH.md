## 1. Zeki et al. 2014 (verified from the article and its supplementary data)
- **Citation:** Semir Zeki, John Paul Romaya, Dionigi M. T. Benincasa, Michael F. Atiyah. "The experience of mathematical beauty and its neural correlates". *Frontiers in Human Neuroscience* 8:68, published 13 February 2014. doi:10.3389/fnhum.2014.00068. Licence: **CC BY** (© 2014 Zeki, Romaya, Benincasa and Atiyah).
- **Affiliations:** Zeki and Romaya: Wellcome Laboratory of Neurobiology, University College London. Benincasa: Department of Physics, Imperial College London. Atiyah: School of Mathematics, University of Edinburgh.
- **Subjects:** "Sixteen mathematicians (3 females, age range = 22–32 years, 1 left-handed) at postgraduate or postdoctoral level, all recruited from colleges in London". S08 was excluded, leaving 15. The scan-time rating was coded −1 Ugly, 0 Neutral, +1 Beautiful, so 0.8667 = 13/15.
- **Procedure:** 60 equations rated at home on −5 (ugly) … 0 (neutral) … +5 (beautiful). The scan came 2–3 weeks later: fMRI on a 3 T Siemens Magnetom Trio. Each equation was shown for 16 s, then the subject pressed Beautiful, Neutral or Ugly. There were 4 sessions of 15 equations each. Understanding was rated afterwards from 0 to 3.
- **Region:** "field A1 of the medial orbito-frontal cortex (mOFC)". Activity rose parametrically with the beauty rating, independently of understanding.
- **Exact results sentence:** "The formula most consistently rated as beautiful (average rating of 0.8667), both before and during the scans, was Leonhard Euler's identity … the one most consistently rated as ugly (average rating of −0.7333) was Srinivasa Ramanujan's infinite series for 1/π".
- **Behavioural data I computed from DataSheet3:**
  - **Eq. 1** `1 + e^{iπ} = 0`: before the scan, mean **+3.6** (highest of the 60). In the scanner: 13 Beau, 2 Neut, 0 Ugly, giving 0.8667.
  - **Eq. 14 (Ramanujan):** before the scan, mean **−1.8** (lowest). In the scanner: 13 Ugly, 2 Beau, giving −0.7333.
  - **Next highest before the scan:** eq. 2 `cos²θ + sin²θ = 1` (3.27), eq. 54 Cauchy–Riemann (3.13), eq. 5 `e^{ix} = cos x + i sin x` (3.0).
  - **Next lowest:** eq. 15 `1729 = 1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³` (−1.13, also Ramanujan-related), eq. 28 `3² + 4² = 5²` (−1.13).
- **Exact Ramanujan formula used (eq. 14, DataSheet1):**
  `1/π = (2√2 / 9801) · Σ_{k=0}^{∞} (4k)! (1103 + 26390k) / ((k!)⁴ · 396^{4k})`
  LaTeX: `\frac{1}{\pi} = \frac{2\sqrt{2}}{9801}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(4k)!\,(1103+26390k)}{(k!)^4\,396^{4k}}`
  The study's description reads "Equation expressing the inverse value of π as an infinite sum". The series is from Ramanujan 1914, "Modular equations and approximations to π" *(memory)*.

## 2. Feynman
- **Born** 11 May 1918 in New York City *(memory, but consistent with Gleick's "a month before he turned fifteen" plus "age of fourteen")*. Grew up in Far Rockaway (Gleick). **Nobel Prize in Physics, 1965.**
- **Notebook:** James Gleick, *Genius: The Life and Science of Richard Feynman* (1992). The plate caption reads "A month before he turned fifteen he covered a page with an elated inch-high scrawl: THE MOST REMARKABLE FORMULA IN MATH. e^{iπ}+1=0 (FROM SCIENCE HISTORY OF THE UNIVERSE)". Later in the book Gleick writes "the assertion he had written elatedly in his notebook at the age of fourteen". **Age 14 is correct.**
- **Where the notebooks are:** the Feynman Papers, Caltech Archives; his high-school maths notebooks are listed in the OAC finding aid. Caltech owns the papers, but the Feynman heirs keep the literary and publication rights. **The notebook page is not free to use.** I did not download it. A pixel recreation, as BUILDERS.md already plans, is the safe route.
- ***Feynman Lectures* vol. I ch. 22 §22-6 "Imaginary exponents" (1963):** "We summarize with this, the most remarkable formula in mathematics: e^{iθ} = cos θ + i sin θ. This is our jewel." I verified this only through secondary quotes, because the Caltech site returned 403. It refers to the general formula.

## 3. Brahmagupta
- Born 598 (possibly at Ujjain) and died c. 670 (MacTutor).
- ***Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta*** ("Correctly established doctrine of Brahma"), 628, written at Bhillamāla (Bhinmal) under the Gurjara rulers, when he was about 30.
- **Zero rules (MacTutor translation):** a number plus or minus zero is unchanged; "A debt minus zero is a debt"; "Zero minus zero is a zero"; zero times anything is zero; "Zero divided by zero is zero" (wrong by modern standards). He wrote n/0 without a value.
- **Portrait:** **no authentic portrait exists.** Every face online is modern and imagined. Commons has a 2024 scrap-metal bas-relief in a Delhi park (CC0); I did not download it, as it is not a likeness. Instead I took a manuscript image: a British Library copy of Pṛthūdhaka's commentary on the *Brāhmasphuṭasiddhānta* (PD).

## 4. Jacob Bernoulli
- Born 6 January 1655 (MacTutor; Commons gives 1654, likely the old-style date, *memory*) in Basel; died 16 August 1705 in Basel. Also spelled Jakob, Jacques or James.
- **1683:** studied compound interest, (1 + 1/n)^n, and showed the limit lies between 2 and 3: the first approximation of e (MacTutor, "The number e"). Euler first used the letter *e* in a 1731 letter to Goldbach, and gave the full treatment in the *Introductio* (1748).

## 5. Imaginary numbers
- **Cardano:** Girolamo (also Gerolamo) Cardano, 1501–1576. ***Ars Magna*** (*Artis magnae sive de regulis algebraicis liber unus*), 1545; printed in Nuremberg *(memory)*. MacTutor quotes: "multiplying 5 + √-15 by 5 - √-15, we obtain 25 - (-15). Therefore the product is 40 … so subtle that it is useless".
- **Bombelli:** Rafael (also Raffaele) Bombelli, 1526–1572. ***L'Algebra***, Bologna, 1572. MacTutor: he called √−n "plus of minus" (Italian *più di meno*, *memory*) and gave multiplication rules, then showed that Cardano's cubic formula gives correct real answers even through square roots of negatives.
- **Descartes:** René Descartes, 1596–1650. ***La Géométrie***, 1637, one of three appendices to the *Discours de la méthode* (Leiden). In Book III he calls such roots *imaginaires* (Wikipedia cites p. 380). I could not retrieve the exact French sentence, so don't put it in quotation marks on screen.
- **Euler** introduced the symbol *i* in 1777 (published 1794; Cajori, not re-checked today). It spread through Gauss.

## 6. Euler
- Leonhard Euler, born 15 April 1707 in Basel; died 18 September 1783 in St Petersburg.
- **Career:** St Petersburg Academy 1727–41, Berlin 1741–66 *(memory)*, St Petersburg again 1766–83 (MacTutor: returned at 59).
- ***Introductio in analysin infinitorum*:** Lausanne (Bousquet), 1748, 2 volumes. Euler's formula is in vol. I, ch. 8 *(memory)*, §138 (verified on the scan).
- **Primary check (Internet Archive scan `introductioanaly00eule`, vol. I p. 104, end of §138):** "Erit vero e^{+v√−1} = cos.v + √−1.sin.v & e^{−v√−1} = cos.v − √−1.sin.v", printed with the old long-s spellings *cof.* and *fin.*.
  - The title page reads "LAUSANNÆ, Apud Marcum-Michaelem Bousquet & Socios, MDCCXLVIII".
  - **Trap for the animation:** on this very page Euler uses the letter ***i* for an infinitely large number**, as in (1 + z/i)^i = e^z, not for √−1. Do not show this page while saying "here is i".
  - Receipts saved: `assets/img/hist_introductio1748_p104_eulers_formula.jpg` and `hist_introductio1748_p104_crop_formula.png`.
- **The identity itself** is not known in Euler's hand (Sandifer 2007; Wilson 2018). It follows from his formula. Wilson says Johann Bernoulli and Roger Cotes came close: Cotes's 1714 logarithmic form, with the date from memory, not re-checked.
- **Eyes:** trouble from 1738, "lost an eye" by 1740; the Handmann portrait of 1753 shows a damaged right eye. Almost totally blind after 1766. A cataract operation in 1771 worked for a few days, then he was totally blind.
- **Output:** almost half his works came after 1766, with help from his sons, Krafft, Lexell and Fuss. He is "the most prolific writer of mathematics of all time" (MacTutor). He wrote about 380 articles in Berlin alone.
- **Portrait:** Jakob Emanuel Handmann, 1753, pastel, Kunstmuseum Basel (PD).

## 7. Mathematical Intelligencer poll
- **Poll:** David Wells, "Which is the most beautiful?", *Math. Intelligencer* 10(4), 1988, pp. 30–31 (doi:10.1007/BF03023741).
- **Results:** David Wells, "Are these the most beautiful?", *Math. Intelligencer* 12(3), 1990, pp. 37–41 (doi:10.1007/BF03024015). There were 76 replies and 68 were used. 24 theorems were scored 0–10.
- **Top results:**
  1. e^{iπ} = −1 (7.7)
  2. V+F=E+2 (7.5)
  3. infinitely many primes (7.5)
  4. 5 regular polyhedra (7.0)
  5. Σ1/n² = π²/6 (7.0)
- **Related:** a *Physics World* reader poll in 2004 (Robert P. Crease) put Euler's identity joint first with Maxwell's equations as "the greatest equation ever".

## 8. Science links
- **Mains:** 50 Hz in India, Europe, most of Asia, Africa and Australia; 60 Hz in North America, much of South America, Saudi Arabia, Taiwan, South Korea and western Japan *(memory)*. CERC's Indian Electricity Grid Code uses a 50 Hz nominal frequency with a tight band (49.90/49.95–50.05 Hz).
- **Fourier transform:** F(f) = ∫ x(t) e^{−2πift} dt. MRI: raw signal = k-space = Fourier samples, and the image comes from an inverse FFT.
- **Schrödinger equation:** iħ ∂ψ/∂t = Ĥψ, with *i* explicit (1926 *(memory)*).
