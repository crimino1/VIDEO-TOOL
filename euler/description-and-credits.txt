DESCRIPTION
What is the most beautiful equation in mathematics? In 2014, scientists in London put mathematicians in a brain scanner and showed them 60 equations. The one they rated most beautiful was tiny: Euler's identity, e^(iπ) + 1 = 0. Richard Feynman called it "the most remarkable formula in math" when he was 14.

In this video you'll understand it from scratch, with nothing beyond what you learned by age 12. Meet the five characters: zero, one, pi (the number of circles), e (the number of growth, found through a bank-interest puzzle) and i (the "imaginary" number that is really just sideways). See why multiplying by i is a quarter turn, why i × i = -1, why growth that points sideways becomes a circle, and why walking halfway around that circle lands you on -1. Then try it yourself, watch the bank trick bend into an arc, and see where Euler's formula e^(ix) = cos x + i sin x shows up in electricity, sound, Wi-Fi, MRI scans and quantum physics.

Start at 1 and walk halfway around a circle. That's the whole equation.
