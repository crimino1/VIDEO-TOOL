DESCRIPTION
What is geometry, really? It began in ancient Egypt, when the Nile flooded every summer and erased the borders of the fields, and rope stretchers walked into the mud to draw them again. The Greeks called it geo-metria: earth measuring.

In this video we build geometry from nothing, the way a five-year-old would: a dot with no size, a line that goes on forever, Euclid's handful of simple rules, angles and why a full turn is 360 degrees, why triangles make bridges strong, why a triangle's angles add up to 180, and the Pythagorean theorem with tiles (3, 4, 5), known in Babylon and in India's Baudhayana Sulbasutra too. Then circles and pi, from Archimedes' 96-sided shapes to Aryabhata's 3.1416, the pizza puzzle (one 18-inch or two 12-inch?), why there are no ants the size of elephants, why bees build hexagons, how Eratosthenes measured the whole Earth with a stick and a shadow, the rule that broke geometry open (Bolyai, Lobachevsky and Gauss), a triangle on the globe with three right angles, Einstein's curved space, and how your phone uses spheres to find you.

It began with ropes stretched across a muddy field. It ended up measuring the universe.
