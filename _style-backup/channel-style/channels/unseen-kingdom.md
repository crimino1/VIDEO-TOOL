## The look
- **Full-screen, one thing at a time:** every shot fills the 16:9 frame, then cuts to the next `[user, seen]`.
- **Mix, from 48 evenly spaced reference frames `[measured]`:** real footage 31%, realistic animal art and animation 35%, ranking board 13%, charts and maps 13%, chapter cards 4%. Target about a third footage, a good third realistic 2D animation, the rest graphics `[user for the animation, measured for the mix]`.
- **Realistic 2D animation `[user]`:** replaces the reference's still illustrations, which only zoom. Animals are shaded with real markings, countershading and light on the skin, not flat cartoons. They move: body wave, tail and flipper motion, drifting particles, light rays, water surface shimmer, layered depth. Nothing on screen is a still for long.
- **Underwater colour:** deep blue and teal, with sunbeams from the surface `[seen 0:14, ~6:45]`.
- **Retired:** the one-camera travelling world and framed clip cards from the shark film. This reference cuts, so they no longer apply `[user]`.

## Video structure
Main reference `[seen, measured]`:
1. **Hook and rules** (about 50s): the question, the animal's abilities, the ranking board.
2. **Rounds, about 90s each, oldest to newest:** chapter card, what the place is like, food, oxygen and temperature, the toughest rival with exact sizes, then the verdict.
3. **Verdict:** back to the board, where the round's tile moves into its row. The board is a scoreboard that fills as the film goes on `[seen ~5:30, ~16:40]`.
4. **Ending:** the full board, then "Do you agree? Do you disagree?" to invite comments `[measured: subtitles at ~18:57]`.

Use the board when the topic is a ranking or a contest. Other topics keep the rounds and verdicts without it `[unknown: user to confirm]`.

## Visual sources
- **All stock is 16:9 and fills the frame `[user]`.** Check real width and height: 1.76 to 1.79 only, at least 1080p where possible. No vertical or square clips, no pillarbox bars, no crop that cuts the subject. Applies to stock video and stock images.
- **Pexels:** search with `orientation=landscape`, then check the ratio. Keys are in `C:\Users\daggu222105\VIDEO-TOOL\.env`.
- **Pinterest:** `pinterest_search` with `aspect` 16:9, using `references/pinterest-footage.md`. 720p at best, rights unknown, so list sources and check rights before monetising `[user, film]`.
- **Real footage carries proof and drama; realistic 2D carries what a camera couldn't film** `[user, seen]`.

- US agency footage (USGS, US Fish and Wildlife) is often free to reuse, but check each clip's terms `[seen]`.

**Copying style, not content:** the reference uses BBC, National Geographic and other documentary clips, plus named artists' illustrations `[seen ~6:30, ~13:50, 1:35]`. Do not take those. Reused documentary footage is a copyright and monetisation risk.

## Text on screen
No word-by-word captions `[seen]`. Text is occasional and short, usually one to four words.

**Chapter card `[seen 1:57 to 2:03]`:** place name in very large heavy condensed type (Anton looks close; League Gothic is already in our projects), white with a thick soft black glow, centred on one or two lines, about 190px at 1080p. On screen about 5s. A smaller line below (about 56px), with a small animal silhouette, gives the date or place, fading in about 2.5s after the title and staying briefly into the next shot.

**Moving label `[seen 0:12 to 0:14]`:** red triangle pointing at the subject, beside one white word in heavy condensed type with a black outline, about 44px at 1080p. It follows the subject through the shot.

**Number stamp `[seen 2:49]`:** a key number such as "38°C", white heavy condensed with a black outline, about 64px, lower centre, fading in over about 0.5s.

**Animal name `[seen ~5:45]`:** same type, lower centre.

## Do and don't
**Do**
- Start on the animal with no channel intro `[seen in all references]`.
- Open on the boldest claim, then prove it in clips `[seen]`.
- Keep every round in the same order, so viewers learn the pattern `[seen]`.
- Turn every big number into a picture of scale `[seen]`.
- Keep the animation moving, even when the shot is calm `[user]`.

**Don't**
- Don't use any stock that isn't 16:9 `[user]`.
- Don't use clips showing a logo, watermark or someone's handle.
- Don't copy the reference's exact board, title layout and orca badge together, because viewers could mistake the two channels.
- Don't use another channel's narration, music or illustrations.
- Don't add word-by-word captions `[seen]`.
- If rendered or AI animals are used, disclose them in the description.
