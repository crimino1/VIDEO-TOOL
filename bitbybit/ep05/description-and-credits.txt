DESCRIPTION
Is your phone listening to you? You talked about hiking boots once, out loud, and an hour later there was an ad for hiking boots. Researchers tested more than 17,000 Android apps and found none secretly sending audio. So how did it know? It never needed to hear you. In this video we build a recommendation system from scratch, one piece at a time, and find out exactly what your feed knows about you.

You'll see how collaborative filtering matches you with strangers who behave like you, the Netflix Prize (one million dollars to beat Netflix's recommendations by 10%, won by 20 minutes), how matrix factorization turns every person and movie into 50 numbers that quietly learn ideas like serious vs silly, why you are a dot in a 50-direction space, how a Wall Street Journal experiment showed the TikTok algorithm figuring out bots from nothing but hesitation, how the YouTube algorithm works in two stages and predicts watch time instead of clicks, and the study where your Facebook likes predicted your personality better than your friends and family.

Then the fair part: the system doesn't understand you, it's arithmetic, and it's trained on what you keep watching, not what you're glad you watched. So here's how to take control of your feed: the not interested button, pausing your watch history, searching on purpose, and finishing what you actually want more of. Plus why that one random video in your feed is the algorithm exploring.

Your phone isn't listening. It's paying attention to what you pay attention to. Bit by bit.
