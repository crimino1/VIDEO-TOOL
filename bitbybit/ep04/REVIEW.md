# EP4 review: Weird Computer Science Concepts, five living computers (9:25)

## What's in it
- 95 shots. 48 new pixel scenes from 6 builders (intro / slime / dnacalc / dnastore / cello / brain), plus EP2's logo sting, which is colour-muted for EP4.
- **Documentary cut (user's request):**
  - chapter cards ("LIVING COMPUTER · No. N", title, amber rule, italic species name)
  - place/year captions
  - the researchers introduced with name tags: Tero & Nakagaki (tags over the real experiment), Adleman, Erlich & Zielinski, Voigt, Kagan
  - a closing line for each chapter ("No brain. No plan. / Still computing.")
- **Calm palette (user's request: no AI-slop colour):** `PX.D` in px.js, bloom 0.2, cream and amber text. No neon, and no glossy 3D stock renders.
- **Real material:**
  - the paper's Tokyo rail experiment movie
  - CC slime mould time-lapses
  - E. coli microscopy
  - the DishBrain paper's own Pong video and neuron images
  - Cortical Labs clips (Kagan, CL1)
  - Pixabay: chip pins, forest floor, LA, NYC, Melbourne, Tokyo trains, lab pipetting, microscope, data hall
- **Sound:** voice as recorded at -14 LUFS, plus 91 soft Pixabay sound effects at -40 LUFS. No music. Final mix -14.0 LUFS / -1.2 dBTP.

## Checked
- Part lengths 146.27 / 123.40 / 149.07 / 146.00 = 564.73 s.

## Facts and wording
- On-screen spellings follow the papers: Adleman (not "Adelman") and Cello (not "Shello").
- **Nuances the narration keeps:** see RESEARCH.md.
  - Learning "within five minutes" was compared against control dishes, not against chance.
  - The $35,000 CL1 price is press-reported.
  - The 800,000 cell count is for the mouse cultures; the human cultures used about 1 million cells.

## Weak spots
- `price_tag` and `power` are simpler than the others. The `kid_wins` figures are small, so the camera pushes in.
- The Kagan and CL1 clips are standard-licence YouTube excerpts, used briefly for commentary and credited on screen.
- Not heard: sound effects were chosen by name and measured, not listened to.
