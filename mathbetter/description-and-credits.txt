DESCRIPTION
How do you get better at math? In 1939, George Dantzig arrived late to class, copied two problems from the board, and solved them, not knowing they were famous unsolved problems in statistics. Nobody had told him they were impossible. This video is for everyone who ever decided "I'm just not a math person."

You'll meet Laurent Schwartz, who thought he was stupid at school and won the Fields Medal, and Maryam Mirzakhani, who struggled at 12 and became the first woman to win it. You'll see what a brain scan revealed about math anxiety (it's the dread, not the math), a 10-minute writing trick tested before real exams, and you'll solve Gauss's famous 1 to 100 problem yourself, then build the formula behind it. Then the science of how to study math: desirable difficulty, testing yourself instead of rereading, the forgetting curve and spaced practice, and why mixed practice beat blocked practice 72% to 38%. You'll finally understand why a negative times a negative is positive, and how Andrew Wiles spent years stuck on Fermat's Last Theorem before solving it.

Being good at math isn't speed. It's spotting the structure, and that's a habit you can build.
