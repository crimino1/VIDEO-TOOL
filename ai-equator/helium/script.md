# Gemini Helium and Google's AGI push (AI Equator, voice: Lucius, eleven_multilingual_v2, no tags, budget 10,000)

## p01 hook
Google just lit up one more box on the periodic table. Helium. And a lot of people are already calling it Google's AGI moment. It isn't. Helium is almost certainly Google's smallest, cheapest Gemini 4 model. But the AGI story around Google is real, and it's moving faster than most people realize. So in this video, I'll show you exactly what Helium is, what the whole Gemini 4 family can already do, what Demis Hassabis is really saying about AGI, and what comes next.

## p02 teaser
Here's how it started. On November 19th, Omar Sanseviero, who works at Google DeepMind, posted three words. Shipping will continue. With a short video of a periodic table, and one element lit up. Helium. About an hour later, TestingCatalog read it as the next Gemini 4 model, and the leaks followed.

## p03 naming
To see why that matters, you need Google's naming system. Every Gemini 4 model is named after an element. Argon came first, in October, and Sundar Pichai says developers picked it up three and a half times faster than Gemini 3. Then Carbon, the most powerful one. Hydrogen, the fast one. And Neon, the middle model, which launched on November 18th. Helium is simply the next box.

## p04 what
So what is it? Google hasn't confirmed anything, but the evidence points one way. Gemini 4 still has no Flash Lite model, the tiny, cheap tier. The current one is Gemini 3.5 Flash Lite, at 30 cents per million input tokens. Helium fills that gap. One leaker claims it launches next week, costs about the same as DeepSeek's cheapest model, and performs close to Claude Opus 5.5. That last part is unverified. But if it's even half true, it's a big deal.

## p05 code
There's also a string in the Gemini web code, tier Helium. But that looks like a subscription tier, not a model. Google even used the name Helium for a cheap plan in India last year. So right now there's no model ID, no benchmarks, and no arena sightings. Just the teaser.

## p06 carbon
Now, the models that are already here, because this is where it gets serious. Gemini 4 Carbon is Google's top model. Koray Kavukcuoglu, who runs Google's AI architecture, said that internally, teams have seen Carbon run for days on a single task. It scored 96.2 percent on GraphWalks, a long context test. And on ARC-AGI-2, the puzzle benchmark built to resist memorization, it scored 94.2 percent.

## p07 carbon2
Carbon didn't even launch to the public at first. Google gave it to a small group of cyber defenders, and only now is it reaching Pro subscribers inside Antigravity, Google's coding tool. It scored 65.2 percent on Terminal Bench 4, which tests real command line work. This is a model built to work, not to chat.

## p08 neon
Neon is the one most people will actually use. Three dollars per million input tokens, a one million token context, number one on the Artificial Analysis coding agent index, and number one on Vending Bench, where a model runs a simulated business. It scored 71.3 percent on the diamond version of Humanity's Last Exam. And Google says it costs 32 percent less per task than Carbon.

## p09 price
Hydrogen is the fast, cheap one, at a dollar fifty, with a free tier. And here's a detail most people missed. On December 1st, Carbon and Argon double in price, from two dollars to four dollars per million input tokens. Neon and Hydrogen stay the same. Google is pushing everyone toward its newest models.

## p10 race
And look at the pace. Anthropic shipped Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9th. OpenAI shipped GPT-6.2 Astra on November 17th, at eight dollars in and forty out. Google answered with Neon the very next day, at three. Three frontier models in nine days. Helium is Google's next move in that race.

## p11 board
On Artificial Analysis right now, Claude Fable 5.2 leads with 65. GPT-6.2 Astra and Gemini 4 Neon are tied at 62, and Carbon has 60. So Google is not number one on raw intelligence. But the betting markets tell a different story. Polymarket gives Google a 78 percent chance of having the best model at the end of 2026. Kalshi's November market has Google at 86.

## p12 arena
That's because Kalshi settles on LMArena, where real people vote on answers without knowing which model wrote them. And right now, that's where Google leads.

## p13 demis
Now, AGI. Watch how Demis Hassabis's words changed this year. In January, at Davos, he was reported saying five to ten years, with about a 50 percent chance by 2030. Then in July, he wrote that AGI is probably only a few short years away, and that it'll be more like the discovery of electricity or fire than another tech product. In October, he told the Wall Street Journal, it's soon, and that he's updating nearer and nearer.

## p14 levels
And DeepMind actually defined this back in 2023, in a paper called Levels of AGI. Instead of one finish line, it lays out steps. Emerging, competent, expert, virtuoso, and superhuman. In that paper, DeepMind called the chatbots of 2023 emerging AGI, the very first step. Today's models have clearly climbed past that. The real question is how far.

## p15 test
He also has a test for it. Train a system only on knowledge from before 1901, and see if it can come up with what Einstein did in 1905. Not answer questions about relativity. Discover it. That's his bar, and it's far higher than any benchmark we have today.

## p16 legg
Shane Legg, DeepMind's co-founder and chief AGI scientist, still gives a 50 percent chance of what he calls minimal AGI by 2028. But he also said it's premature to declare that AGI has been achieved. That was a direct answer to executives at OpenAI and Nvidia. And Sundar Pichai put it more bluntly. Whether you and I call it AGI or not doesn't matter, because it'll be very, very powerful.

## p17 openai
Because OpenAI already planted the flag. When GPT-6 Astra launched in September, Greg Brockman said, welcome to the AGI era. And GPT-6.2 Astra was just reported as the first model to score 100 percent on ARC-AGI-3. Carbon scored 65 on the standard version.

## p18 chollet
But the man who built ARC, François Chollet, says solving it is not proof of AGI. It shows a model can pick up new puzzles fast. That's a skill, not general intelligence. And I agree with him. Benchmarks are getting beaten faster than anyone can build new ones. Gary Marcus still says these models are not AGI, and Yann LeCun calls the idea that scaling language models gets us there nonsense.

## p19 rsi
So why is Google so confident? On the last earnings call, Sundar Pichai said he expects continued acceleration because of RSI. Recursive self improvement. AI that helps build the next AI. And OpenAI says GPT-6.2 Astra is the first model it has shipped that meets its own high threshold for AI self improvement. That's the real story here. Not one model. The loop.

## p20 compute
And Google has the hardware to run that loop. Its eighth generation TPUs, announced in April, come in two versions, one for training and one for inference. A single training pod has 9,600 chips and 121 exaflops, almost three times Ironwood. Google is spending around 180 billion dollars on infrastructure this year. The Gemini app just passed a billion monthly users. Google Cloud revenue more than doubled, and Google's own model APIs now handle around 32 billion tokens every minute. Google has even launched TPUs into space, to test running AI in orbit.

## p21 why
So where does Helium fit? At the opposite end. If Carbon is the model that runs for days, Helium is the one that runs everywhere. Cheap enough for every phone, every app, every agent. And that's how this level of capability actually reaches people. Not through the most expensive model, but through the cheapest one that's good enough.

AGI won't arrive as one big announcement. Logan Kilpatrick from Google said it'll look a lot like a product release. I think he's right. It's arriving one release at a time.
