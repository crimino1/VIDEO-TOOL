# VIDEO 1: voice script (about 5 minutes)

**Title options**
- AI Just Lost Almost $3 Trillion. Then Sam Altman Became RAM Altman.
- The AI Crash and the RAM Altman Disaster

**Read this:**

On Thursday, the AI trade had its worst day in years. About two and a half trillion dollars, gone in a single session. Some counts put it close to three trillion.

And just one day earlier, the CEO of OpenAI changed his name on X to Ram Altman.

These two stories are more connected than you think.

Let's start with the crash. On November fifth, the Nasdaq fell five and a half percent, its worst day since April of last year. The Nasdaq 100 had its worst day since March of 2020.

Chip stocks took the biggest hit. A closely watched index of chipmakers fell twelve percent, its worst day in about six and a half years. Nvidia dropped more than nine percent, wiping out all of its gains for the year. Tesla fell eleven percent. SpaceX, nine.

CoreWeave, one of the companies that rents out AI computing power, fell another ten percent. It's now down seventy-six percent from its peak last year.

Wall Street's fear gauge jumped about seventy percent. Then the panic went global. The next morning, South Korea's market plunged almost nine percent at the open, and trading was halted for twenty minutes. By the close, South Korea and Japan were both down six percent.

So what happened? Did AI suddenly stop working?

Not exactly. The trigger was politics. Democrats won big in the US midterms, and investors got scared that they could slow down the data center boom.

But that fear only works because so much of the market now rests on one bet. That companies will keep pouring hundreds of billions of dollars into chips and data centers, and that all of it will eventually pay off. And nobody can tell you exactly when.

There was another warning sign that week. Qualcomm missed estimates, and said higher memory chip costs are hurting its sales. Its stock fell more than nine percent.

Which brings us to memory. And to Sam.

A few days earlier, Sam Altman went on the Cheeky Pint podcast with Stripe co-founder John Collison. And he said that, from a global allocation perspective, it's "quite terrible" that everyone keeps a piece of the world's limited memory supply in their own computer, where it's used just "five percent of the time."

Now think about who said that.

AI data centers have bought up so much memory that one research firm expects them to use more than seventy percent of high-end memory chips this year. Early this year, regular memory contract prices jumped around ninety percent in a single quarter. Phones, laptops, even smart home devices are getting more expensive.

And the man building some of the biggest AI data centers on Earth just called your RAM the waste.

The clip passed two point four million views, and the internet did what the internet does. People pointed out that your car sits parked most of the day too. Someone made RAM stick earrings. And Elon Musk posted that the people deserve to have their RAM too.

So Sam did the only thing left. Someone on X asked him to change his name. He said, "OK yeah." And for a brief, beautiful moment, the CEO of OpenAI was Ram Altman.

Then he posted, "I want you to have so much RAM. Let's build more." He also said the comment was taken out of context, and called that "damn annoying."

To be fair to him, his point was about scarcity. When supply is short, shared computers in the cloud can get more use out of every chip. And he says he has been arguing for more memory production for a long time.

But you can't build a memory factory with a tweet. SK Hynix, one of the biggest memory makers in the world, says this shortage could last until 2030.

Now here's the part that matters for you. On Friday, the market bounced. The Nasdaq rose about one percent. Nvidia, almost three. SpaceX, more than eight. And Wall Street veteran Ed Yardeni told NBC he doesn't think this is the AI bubble bursting.

But even after that bounce, the S&P 500 finished its worst week since April of last year.

So did the AI bubble pop? The honest answer is that one bad week can't tell you that. The spending is still happening. The demand for computing is still real.

But now we know how nervous all that money is. One election, one bad day, and two and a half trillion dollars disappears.

And here's the funniest part. AI stocks can crash by trillions, and your RAM still doesn't get any cheaper.

If you want the next one of these, subscribe. This has been AI Equator. I'll see you in the next one.

# VIDEO 2: voice script (about 4 minutes)

**Title options**
- The Best AI Models Right Now, and What's Coming Next
- Every AI Model That Matters This Month

**Read this:**

Right now, the smartest AI model in the world, according to one of the most watched leaderboards, is one you probably can't use.

And by the end of this month, that list could look completely different. So let's go through who's actually winning, what's coming next, and what people are really building with these things.

Start with the scoreboard. On the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, Google's Gemini 4 Carbon sits at number one with a score of sixty. Claude Opus 5.5 is right behind it at fifty-nine. And GPT-6 Astra is close at fifty-seven.

That's three companies, separated by three points.

But one score doesn't tell you everything. SemiAnalysis just ran two hundred and six real work tasks, across coding, finance, law, and biology. This time, Claude Opus 5.5 came first, and Carbon came second by a tiny gap.

Then look at the price. Opus costs about fifty-one dollars a task. Astra, about fifty-four. Carbon, just over sixteen.

There's one catch. Google has only released Carbon to a small group of cyber defenders, with wider access promised soon. So the best deal in AI is one most of us can't touch yet.

Now, the older sibling you can use today is Gemini 4 Argon, and people have been pushing it hard. One person asked it for a full tour of the solar system in a single file, and says it cost twenty-eight cents. Another built a little open-world game in Unity, though he was honest that the result wasn't anything special.

That's a good rule for all of these demos. Look at what people actually made, not just what they claim.

So what's coming next? And this part comes with a warning, because almost all of it is rumor.

OpenAI is the one everyone's watching. Some users have spotted something called GPT-6.2 Astra in their Codex usage logs, but OpenAI hasn't announced it. What OpenAI did release, on November fifth, is GPT-6 Cyber, a model built for security work.

Anthropic is rumored to be working on new models too, with names like Opus 6 and Fable 5.5 being passed around. Nothing is confirmed.

Google has Carbon's wider release still to come, and reports point to a Gemini Omni Pro model for 4K video.

Then there's Grok 5, probably the most anticipated model that still hasn't shipped. xAI said it was in training back in January. It still hasn't committed to a date, and smaller Grok updates are now rumored to come first.

Meanwhile, DeepSeek just keeps shipping, and new versions are rumored for later this month.

So here's the real story. A year ago, you'd wait months for a new frontier model. Google just released two in two weeks.

The top of this list can change before you finish your coffee. Which is why the number one spot matters less than you think. For most people, the question isn't which model is smartest. It's which one you can actually use, what it costs, and whether it finishes the job.

We'll keep tracking it. Subscribe, and I'll tell you when these rumors turn real.

This has been AI Equator. I'll see you in the next one.
