Sam Altman Rebrands as RAM Altman and Says He Wants You to 'Have So Much RAM'

Sam Altman Rebrands as RAM Altman and Says He Wants You to 'Have So Much RAM'
Jean Leon |Nov 5, 2026
Add Android Headlines as a preferred source on Google

SUMMARY

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman rebranded his social media profile to RAM Altman following intense backlash over podcast comments questioning personal PC memory usage, while exact textual quotes from his posts confirm he is now urging manufacturers to ramp up global DRAM production.

OpenAI boss Sam Altman learned quickly that telling PC gamers they do not need computer memory is a bad move. After arguing on a podcast that keeping RAM inside desktop computers is wasteful, the internet came for him. To stop the online bleeding, Sam Altman renamed his account on X to RAM Altman and jumped into damage control by saying that he wants you “to have so much RAM.”

Video Muted

The controversy kicked off during an interview on the Cheeky Pint podcast. There, Altman complained about limited DRAM supplies sitting in home computers. He claimed that personal computers only run at full speed 5% of the time, making local memory inefficient for the global supply chain. In his own words, Altman said, “From a global allocation perspective, actually, it’s quite terrible to have everybody have a bunch of the world’s limited supply of DRAM sitting in their own computer.”

It’s noteworthy that, at the time of writing this story, OpenAI CEO already restored its usual name on X. However, the change was immortalized in screenshots.

Gamers and tech rivals mock cloud memory push

PC builders and gamers instantly pushed back, accusing AI companies of trying to grab consumer hardware for cloud data centers. Elon Musk joined the dogpile on social media, posting a movie meme declaring that regular people deserve to keep their RAM too.

Altman tried to defuse the heat by posting on X, “I actually do want the world to have tons of memory and people to put as much as they want in their computers.”

Hoping to play along with the joke, Altman officially changed his display name to RAM Altman to calm angry followers. He followed up with another post on X stating, “I want you to have so much RAM. Let’s build more.” However, telling manufacturers to build more chips does not solve the immediate shortage facing consumer retail stores.

Severe DRAM shortages keep retail hardware expensive

Major chipmakers like Samsung and SK Hynix keep devoting their production lines to enterprise high-bandwidth memory for AI servers. Industry analysts expect consumer DRAM prices to stay high for years while hyperscalers snap up available chip supplies. That tight supply chain leaves everyday PC builders paying premium prices regardless of Altman’s social media rebrand.

The fight playing out online points to real tensions between the massive growth of AI infrastructure and the needs of everyday consumer tech. Voters and local leaders are already closely watching how power grids and component supplies are being strained by data center expansions. Changing a username on X makes for funny headlines, but fixing global hardware bottlenecks takes much more than clever PR.
