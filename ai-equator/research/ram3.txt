Sam Altman backtracks on RAM comments

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman suggested last week that more of the world’s RAM supply should go to AI data centers. Now he’s walking that back.

listen
Print view

(Image: FotoField / Shutterstock.com)

Nov 5, 2026 at 2:10 pm CET
2 min. read
By
Andreas Floemer

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman responded Wednesday to criticism of his comments about how RAM should be used. He even temporarily changed his name on X to “RAM Altman” and said users should put as much RAM in their PCs as they want.

The controversy started with a comment by the OpenAI CEO on Stripe co-founder John Collison’s “Cheeky Pint” podcast. He said that, from the perspective of global resource allocation, it was actually pretty terrible “that everyone has a portion of the world’s limited DRAM supply sitting in their computer — where it’s actually used only 5% of the time.” Critics saw this as a proposal to put more of the world’s RAM supply into data centers.

He said on X it was “damn annoying” that his comment had been taken out of context. “I actually want there to be tons of memory in the world and for people to be able to put as much as they want in their computers.” He added, “I’ve been advocating for a long time for memory production to be ramped up globally.”

In response to another comment on X, he said, “I want you to have that much RAM. Let’s build more.”

Memory shortages also linked to OpenAI

Operators of AI data centers have bought up a large share of the world’s RAM supply, triggering a shortage. This is driving up prices for products such as smartphones, laptops, and even smart home devices. The memory crisis is unlikely to ease anytime soon. Market watchers expect it to last at least through next year, and SK Hynix predicts it could stretch through 2030. The analysts at Gartner and Counterpoint don’t expect relief before 2028, when new manufacturing capacity comes online.

OpenAI and its co-founder Sam Altman are considered among the main contributors to the shortages. Altman began approaching chipmakers and investors in late 2023 to raise $7 trillion for 36 new chip factories and additional data centers. That effort failed; construction of new AI data centers is also increasingly stalling.
