Calculus 1 Full Course, Episode 2: Limits, the idea the whole of calculus is built on. In 23 minutes you'll learn what a limit really is, why a function with a hole still has a limit, why the limit ignores the value at the point, one-sided limits, when a limit does not exist, the limit laws, direct substitution, and the 3 keys that unlock every 0/0 problem: factoring, simplifying fractions inside fractions, and the conjugate trick. 8 practice questions solved step by step, and the last one secretly turns out to be your very first derivative.

This series covers the full Calculus 1 / AP Calculus AB syllabus, step by step, from the basics to the final exam.

WHAT YOU'LL LEARN
- What a limit means: where a function is heading as x gets closer and closer to a number
- Limit notation, and why a hole (open circle) doesn't stop a limit
- Why the limit doesn't care about the actual value f(a), only the approach
- One-sided limits from the left and right, and when the full limit exists
- The 3 ways a limit fails to exist: jumps, blowing up to infinity, wild wiggling
- Limit laws and direct substitution for nice functions
- Solving 0/0 limits with factoring, compound fractions and the conjugate
- A step-by-step game plan for any limit problem

PRACTICE QUESTIONS (pause and try them in the video)
1. A graph heads to 3 as x approaches 5, with a hole there and a dot at (5, 8). Find the limit and f(5)
2. Left limit at x = 3 is 7, right limit is 7. Does the limit exist?
3. Left limit at x = 1 is -2, right limit is 4. Does the limit exist?
4. lim x->5 of (2x^2 - 3x)
5. lim x->1 of (x^2 + 3x - 4)/(x - 1)
6. lim x->9 of (sqrt(x) - 3)/(x - 9)
7. A graph heads to 4 from both sides as x approaches 2, but f(2) = 1. Find the limit
8. Challenge: lim x->0 of ((x + 3)^2 - 9)/x
