# Unseen Kingdom 2 editing style

Named by the user on 2026-11-15: "name this style Unseen Kingdom 2" `[user]`. Built from two reference videos by the same creator as the first Unseen Kingdom reference (the orca prehistoric video). The user wants the same editing style at **slightly higher quality than the reference** `[user]`. Still a draft until a test edit is approved.

**Two formats, never mixed:** the long-form 16:9 ranking film (every section from Summary to Build notes) and the 9:16 short (the "Shorts (9:16)" block near the end, added 2026-11-22). Numbers and rules from one do not apply to the other.

Marks: `[measured]`, `[seen 3:20]`, `[user]`, `[unknown: what would settle it]`.

## Summary
Faceless "one animal (or you) against every era" ranking documentaries. One question in the hook, then 12 to 14 rounds, each opened by a huge white title on a blurred dark frame and closed by dropping a tile onto a tier board. Pictures alternate between full-screen documentary footage and a single framed picture floating on a blurred, darkened copy of itself. Straight cuts about every 5 seconds, a casual first-person narrator, almost no text.

## Video structure
- **Rounds:** 14 in Polar Bear, 12 in Ocean. 45s to 2m20s each, most 70 to 100s `[measured from narration pauses]`.
- **Every round, same order `[seen, measured]`:**
  1. Title card, 5 to 8s, no narration (pauses measured at 5.1 to 11.0s).
  2. Intro line: "First up is...", "Next is...", "Now you're floating in the...". Where and when it lived.
  3. Size and weapons with exact numbers, each number on its own picture.
  4. A size comparison chart with a human silhouette.
  5. The fight or the danger, talked through like a friend arguing it out.
  6. Verdict on the tier board: the tile is dragged into its row, held about 5s.
  7. Fade to black, next card.
- **Variety beats, once or twice a film:** a "Mystery Round" card with a spin wheel choosing the animal `[seen 9:45 Polar]`; a short retro pixel-art animation `[seen ~8:30 Ocean]`; a joke note on screen in asterisks, "*I couldn't put it up there... just didn't make sense*" `[seen 19:10 Ocean]`.
- **Ending:** last tile placed, the finished board zooms in to fill the screen over about 2s, "So there's the list. Do you agree? Do you disagree? Let me know." Fade to black. No end screen, no subscribe card `[seen, measured]`.

## Shots and camera
- Full-screen footage plays at normal speed, untouched, no zoom added `[seen]`.
- **Framed picture `[seen 3:36 Ocean, 0:54 Polar]`:** the picture sits centred at about 75% of frame width (portrait pictures at about 70% of frame height), sharp edges, a thin soft dark shadow, no border. Behind it, the same picture scaled to fill, heavily blurred and darkened to roughly 25% brightness. It pushes in slowly, about 1.5% over 3.5s. Held 3 to 6s.
- Wide artwork that is already 16:9 runs full-screen with the same slow push `[seen]`.
- Opening shot is always the tightest, most intense frame available (eyes, teeth) `[seen 0:00]`.

## Transitions
- Default: straight cut `[seen]`.
- Into a round: fade to black about 0.5s, black held about 0.5s with silence, card fades up about 0.5s, holds 3 to 4s, fades to black 0.5s, first shot of the round fades up 0.5s `[seen 0:20 to 0:29 Ocean]`.
- Board to card: the board fades to black about 0.5s `[seen 7:20 Polar]`.
- Nothing else. No whip pans, no glitches, no zoom transitions.

## Colour and texture
- Footage keeps its own grade. No LUT, grain, vignette or letterbox `[seen]`.
- Dark surfaces: title card background and framed-picture background are the next shot blurred and darkened; board background is near-black `#1a1a1a` `[seen]`.
- Ocean film sits in teal, deep blue and murky green; Ice Age film in snow white, tan grass and brown fur `[seen]`.

## Text on screen
No word-by-word captions. About one text element every 30 to 60 seconds `[seen]`.

- **Round title card `[seen 0:23 Ocean, 0:34 Polar]`:** Anton (or a near match), white, sentence case as written ("Giant Long Horned Bison"), about 200px at 1080p, centred, one or two lines, with a thick soft black glow all round (about 12px blur, doubled). Background is the round's first shot, blurred and darkened to about 30%. Fades in from grey over 0.5s.
- **Date or size stamp `[seen 0:29 Ocean, ~11:15 Polar]`:** same font, about 64px, white with black glow, lower centre about 8% up from the bottom. "635 - 485 Million Years Ago", "3.7 - 4 Meters / 40 KG". Fades in over about 1s, 1 to 2s after the shot starts, stays to the cut.
- **Source credit `[seen]`:** white serif (Times-like), about 22px, bottom-left corner, on footage and artwork that needs it.
- **Joke note `[seen 19:10 Ocean]`:** same Anton, about 72px, left aligned, wrapped in asterisks, over the board.

## Graphics

## Do and don't
**Do**
- Open on the tightest, most intense shot, with the claim in the first sentence.
- Give every number its own picture.
- Keep every round in the same order so viewers learn the pattern.
- Drop to black and silence before each title card.
- Let the narrator have opinions and say "I".
- End on the finished board and the "Do you agree?" question.

**Don't**
- Don't use BBC, Nat Geo, Prehistoric Planet or other documentary footage, or artists' pictures without a free licence.
- Don't add captions, lower thirds, progress bars or a channel intro.
- Don't use fancy transitions.
- Don't copy the reference's exact board site look, row wording and title treatment all together; viewers could confuse the channels.
- Don't hold a framed picture without the slow push.

- Cut counts understate the speed. Most change happens inside a shot: something new enters, swaps pose or punches in about every 0.5 to 1s `[seen 0:00 to 0:04 Survive: bear, moose, alligator, cassowary, one each half second]`.

### Hook (first 3 seconds)
- The question is the first sentence, and it promises the top of the list: "Which animal has the strongest bite force and can snap a car in half?", "How deep does the ocean actually go?" `[measured]`.
- Under it, a preview: several list items flash past the host in 2s `[seen 0:00 Survive]`, or the host appears in the opening place `[seen 0:00 Deep, Bite]`.
- A one-line joke, then "First off," and item one by 0:04 `[measured]`.

### Structure
- A list climbing from weakest to strongest (Bite: coyote to megalodon, 17 items), shallowest to deepest (Deep: 12 depth stops), or easiest to hardest (Survive: 6 animals) `[measured]`.
- One sentence per item: the name, then one concrete picture of what it can do ("crack a coconut", "the height of the Eiffel Tower").
- A host joke about every 15s, at their own expense `[measured]`.
- Ending: a twist item (humans, trash left by humans) or the biggest item, then a 2s gag and sign-off ("Don't go outside", "Subscribe to save me") `[measured, seen]`. No end card.

### Picture
- **Layers, back to front `[seen]`:** a photo background, softly blurred; ground shadow ellipses; the cut-out animals and props; the host; sometimes a blurred foreground strip (grass) for depth.
- **Animal cut-outs:** photoreal, clean edges, 2 or 3 poses each swapped on the beat (mouth shut, mouth open, lunge). They slide, hop and rotate as whole pieces; no limb animation `[seen 0:00 to 0:04 Survive]`.
- **Focus pull:** when a prop matters, the host and background blur and the prop is sharp, then it reverses `[seen 0:20 Bite]`.
- **Punch-in:** a cut to a tight close-up of the host or animal with white speed lines round the edge, about 0.5s `[seen 0:03 Survive]`.
- **Running prop per video:** one device that makes the list measurable. Bite: a bite sensor on a stand with an LED screen whose number spins up and settles ("34, 358, 350 PSI") `[seen 0:20 Bite]`. Deep: a submarine with the host in the porthole, sinking through one tall scrolling scene whose water darkens from bright blue to black, with real objects placed at their depth `[seen]`. Survive: the host is chased `[seen]`.
- **Small accents:** white tick marks bursting round an object under pressure `[seen 0:13 Deep]`; a cartoon blank-faced person as the "average human" `[seen 0:07 Bite]`.
- Backgrounds change with the animal's home (lab, lawn, desert, snow, forest path, swamp) `[seen]`.

### The animation-only rule
- No filmed person anywhere in a short `[user]`.
- The host is **our own original animated character**. Do not recreate the reference host: no bald head with safety goggles, white lab coat and orange shirt. That look is a real creator's identity.
- Proposed default `[unknown: user verdict on a look test]`: a cartoon host from the approved flat rig (`flat-test\assets\js\flat.js`, `FL.person`: walks, points, waves, blinks), given a signature outfit and expressive face poses (shock, smug, scared), standing in the same photo-collage world. Earlier lesson: the first simple 2D person was rejected as very bad, so the host needs a real face and proportions before any film is built.
- Animals and props stay as cut-out pictures, from free-licence photos (Wikimedia Commons, Pexels) cut with `uk2-kit\tools\cut.py` (HyperFrames background remover), never from the reference. Matching poses of one animal (mouth shut, mouth open) are hard to find in free photos, so test that swap first `[unknown: test result]`.
- The "average human" stand-in and any victims are drawn figures too.

### Shorts do and don't
**Do:** ask the question in the first sentence; put something new on screen every second; give each video one running prop; cut every pause; end on a twist and a joke.

**Don't:** film or composite a real person; copy the reference host's look, catchphrases ("bro") or exact jokes; reuse its scripts word for word; carry long-form rules (175 wpm, black-and-silence pauses) into a short.
